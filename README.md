## Design 1: "The Cyber Sentinel" (Modern & Tech-Focused)
**Concept**: A sleek, dark-themed aesthetic that emphasizes your security background. Instead of a standard wave, this uses a "soft" or "slice" edge with a high-contrast cyber-blue/purple gradient. It looks more "tech" and less "playful" than the standard wave.

## 🧠 Expert Design Tips ("Adding My Knowledge")

1.  **Color Psychology in Security**:
    *   **Blue (`#0072ff`)**: Represents trust, intelligence, and stability. Good for "Blue Team" vibes.
    *   **Red (`#EA4335`)**: Represents energy, urgency, and offense. Good for "Red Team" or strong CTA areas.
    *   **Purple (`#8E2DE2`)**: Often associated with "SecOps" (combining Red and Blue) and premium tech brands.
    *   **Green (`#0F9D58`)**: "System Nominal", growth, and success.

2.  **Consistency**:
    *   Ensure your banner colors match your GitHub stats cards (the ones at the bottom of your profile). Currently, your stats cards use a mix of transparent and flat themes. I recommend unifying them. For example, add `&theme=radical` or `&theme=tokyonight` to all your stat card URLs to match a dark-themed banner.

3.  **Call to Action (CTA)**:
    *   A banner captures attention, but what do you want them to do next? Place your most important badged links (LinkedIn, Portfolio) *immediately* under the banner (as you have done), but ensure they contrast well against the background if they were *inside* the banner image.

4.  **Whitespace**:
    *   Don't overfill the banner. Your name and a focused tagline (e.g., "Cybersecurity -> DevSecOps") is perfect. Too much text makes it look cluttered on mobile devices.
